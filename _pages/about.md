I am an applied microeconomist and PhD Candidate at Freie Universität Berlin, working at the Chair of Empirical Economics and Gender under the supervision of Prof. Natalia Danzer and Prof. Peter Haan. I am also an Affiliated Doctoral Researcher at DIW Berlin and a Netspar Junior Fellow.

Before starting my PhD, I spent several years working in policy making within the German Parliament and federal ministries. That experience shapes how I approach research today: I am drawn to questions with clear policy relevance, and I care as much about translating findings for policymakers and the public as I do about the underlying empirical work.

My research sits at the intersection of labor, pension, and gender economics, with a particular focus on Germany's statutory pension system. I study the fiscal and distributional effects of pension reforms, including the abolition of penalty-free early retirement and contribution-year based eligibility rules, and how these reforms have heterogeneous effects across groups, especially women and older workers. A second line of my work examines gender gaps in numeracy and their relationship to personality traits.
