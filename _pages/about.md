I am an applied microeconomist and PhD Candidate at the Free University of Berlin, working at the Chair of Empirical Economics and Gender under the supervision of Prof. Natalia Danzer and Prof. Peter Haan. I am also an Affiliated Doctoral Researcher at DIW Berlin and a Netspar Junior Fellow.

Before starting my PhD, I spent several years working in policy making within the German Parliament and federal ministries. That experience shapes how I approach research today: I am drawn to questions with clear policy relevance, and I care as much about translating findings for policymakers and the public as I do about the underlying empirical work.

My research sits at the intersection of labor, pension, and gender economics, with a particular focus on Germany's statutory pension system. I study the fiscal and distributional effects of pension reforms, including the abolition of penalty-free early retirement and contribution-year based eligibility rules, and how these reforms affect different groups differently, especially women and older workers. A second line of my work examines gender gaps in numeracy and their relationship to personality traits. Methodologically, I rely on quasi-experimental designs, including difference-in-differences, regression discontinuity, instrumental variables, and bunching estimation, applied to German administrative pension microdata.

Several of my policy studies, conducted jointly with colleagues at DIW Berlin and commissioned by the Bertelsmann Stiftung, have received wide media coverage in outlets including DIE ZEIT, Der Spiegel, Handelsblatt, and Tagesschau, and I have discussed this work in TV and radio interviews.
